# 1. Philosophy

A lesson is not a page.

A lesson is an experience.

Learners should spend more time interacting than reading.

Every lesson should feel like a conversation between the learner and the application rather than reading a textbook.

The objective is understanding through interaction.

---

# 2. Learning Objectives

Every lesson must answer three questions.

## What will I learn?

Clearly state the learning objective.

Example

> Learn where the letter ب originates.

---

## Why is it important?

Explain why the concept matters in Quran recitation.

---

## What will I be able to do afterwards?

Describe the practical outcome.

Example

> You will correctly pronounce the letter ب from its proper place of articulation.

---

# 4. Lesson Stages

## Introduction

Purpose

Introduce today's topic.

Length

30–60 seconds.

Should answer

"What am I learning today?"

---

## Learning Goal

Clearly define the expected outcome.

The learner should understand exactly what success looks like before beginning.

---

## Concept

Explain the concept using simple language.

Avoid large paragraphs.

Break explanations into small pieces.

Whenever possible, accompany explanations with:

- diagrams
- illustrations
- audio
- animations

---

## Examples

Provide multiple examples.

Recommended progression

Simple

↓

Medium

↓

Real Quran Example

Examples should gradually increase in difficulty.

---

## Guided Practice

Allow learners to practice immediately.

Practice should focus on one skill only.

Avoid introducing new concepts during practice.

---

## Recording

The learner records their own recitation.

Recording should always happen after guided practice.

---

## AI Feedback

The AI analyzes the recording.

Feedback should include:

- what was correct
- what was incorrect
- where the mistake occurred
- why it happened
- how to improve

Feedback should remain encouraging and constructive.

---

## Retry

The learner immediately applies the feedback.

Multiple retries should always be allowed.

---

## Assessment

Assess whether the learner understood today's lesson.

Assessment may include:

- pronunciation
- identification
- listening
- multiple choice
- matching
- practical recitation

Assessment should measure understanding, not memorization.

---

## Summary

Briefly review today's lesson.

Focus on:

- what was learned
- key takeaway
- common mistake

---

## Completion

Celebrate progress.

Clearly communicate:

- lesson completed
- progress made
- next recommended lesson

# 5. Lesson Design Principles

Every lesson should:

- Teach one primary concept.
- Keep explanations concise.
- Encourage interaction.
- Allow immediate practice.
- Provide immediate feedback.
- End with a measurable outcome.

---

# 6. Reading Guidelines

Reading should support learning, not replace it.

Avoid:

- long paragraphs
- textbook-style explanations
- unnecessary theory

Prefer:

- short explanations
- visual learning
- demonstrations
- examples

---

# 7. Practice Guidelines

Practice should always follow explanation.

Learners should never be expected to understand a concept without first applying it.

Practice should become gradually more challenging throughout the lesson.

---

# 8. AI Feedback Guidelines

The AI should never simply state:

"Incorrect."

Instead, feedback should explain:

- where the mistake occurred
- why the mistake occurred
- how to correct it
- allow another attempt

Feedback should encourage improvement rather than discourage mistakes.

---

# 9. Completion Criteria

A lesson is considered completed when:

- Every required learning section has been viewed.
- Guided practice has been attempted.
- At least one recording has been submitted.
- The assessment has been completed.

Completion indicates participation.

It does not necessarily indicate mastery.

---

# 10. Mastery

Mastery is achieved through repeated success.

A learner reaches mastery when they consistently demonstrate accurate performance over multiple practice sessions.

Mastery is separate from lesson completion.

---

# 11. Accessibility

Every lesson should support:

- RTL layouts
- Audio-first learning
- Clear typography
- High contrast
- Keyboard accessibility (where applicable)
- Mobile-first interaction

---

# 12. Future Compatibility

This blueprint should support future educational systems including:

- Qaida
- Tajweed
- Hifz
- Advanced Recitation
- Future learning modules

without requiring changes to the lesson structure.

---

# 13. Guiding Principle

Every lesson should leave the learner feeling that they have genuinely improved.

The learner should never leave a lesson wondering:

"What was I supposed to learn?"

Instead, they should leave knowing:

- what they learned,
- why it matters,
- how to apply it,
- and what to learn next.
