lesson - 02 Alphabet shapes

description:
The "Alphabet Shapes" lesson isn't teaching words. It's teaching how Arabic letters change shape depending on where they appear (beginning, middle, end, or isolated). When you see something like لح, you don't read it as laḥ. You read each letter separately:

لح → Lām + Ḥā (لام + حاء)
با → Bā + Alif (باء + ألف)
كب → Kāf + Bā (كاف + باء)

The app is showing common letter combinations so you recognize the different forms of each letter in connected writing. As the page says, focus on the shape and the dots, not the pronunciation as a word.

lesson 03 - Harakat

description:
Harakat
Avoid stretching or jerking the Fatḥah (ـَ), Kasrah (ـِ), or Ḍammah (ـُ).
For example:
بَ should be read ba, not "baa".
بِ should be read bi, not "bii".
بُ should be read bu, not "buu".
Stretching these short vowels (Fatḥah, Kasrah, and Ḍammah) results in incorrect recitation.
Fatḥah (ـَ) and Ḍammah (ـُ) are written above the letter, while Kasrah (ـِ) is written below the letter.
Pronounce Fatḥah by opening the lips naturally and straight.
Avoid opening the mouth too wide when pronouncing Fatḥah.
Pronounce Kasrah by lowering the lips slightly downward.
Pronounce Ḍammah by rounding the lips completely.
Avoid unclear (majhool) pronunciation of Kasrah and Ḍammah.
Summary
Fatḥah (ـَ) → a (short "a")
Kasrah (ـِ) → i (short "i")
Ḍammah (ـُ) → u (short "u")

All three are short vowels and should be pronounced briefly, without stretching them into ā, ī, or ū.

lesson 04 - long voices

description:
Long voices should be elongated equal to one Alif.
Long voices are the sounds of the Madd letters
a vertical Fatḥah (Alif Maddah)
a vertical Kasrah (Yā Maddah)
an inverted Ḍammah (Wāw Maddah)
Long voices carry the same lengthening as Madd letters.
Do not stretch long voices beyond one Alif in length.
Making a long voice shorter than one Alif leads to an incomplete recitation.

lesson 05 - Tanween

description:
Double Fatha (an), Double Kasrah (in), and Double Dammah (un) are called Tanween.
Letters with Tanween are pronounced with a Noon sound at the end.
Pronounce Tanween the same way as a Noon Sakin. For example, بً is read بَنْ (BĀN) and بٍ is read بِنْ (BIN).

lesson 06 - Sukoon

description:
Sukoon always appears above a letter.
A letter with Sukoon is joined with the previous letter in recitation, like KHALQAN (خَلْقًا).
While pronouncing Sukoon, do not do bouncing echo (Qalqalah) on any letter except the Qalqalah letters: Qaf (ق), Ta (ط), Ba (ب), Jim (ج), and Daal (د).

lesson 07 - Shaddah
description:
Connect the previous letter to the Shaddah letter with pressure.
A letter with Shaddah is pronounced twice; once joined with the previous letter (sakin), and once with its vowel (mutaharrik), e.g. TABBA = TAB + BA (تَبَّ).
When Noon or Meem has a Shaddah, perform one Alif nasal sound (Ghunnah), e.g. INNA (إِنَّ), AMMA (عَمَّ).
If Shaddah is on a heavy letter, do not make its first sound light, e.g. UṬṬILAT (عُطِّلَتْ).
Avoid stretching Waw and Ya when they have Shaddah, e.g. AWWALA (أَوَّلَ), QAYYIMATUN (قَيِّمَةٌ).
When stopping at a letter with Shaddah, take the time of two letters, e.g. TABB (تَبّ), AL-MAFARR (الْمَفَرّ), AL-MASS (الْمَسّ).

lesson 08 - Qalqalah Letters

description:
These are the letters pronounced with qalqalah (a bouncing echo sound).
There are five qalqalah letters: Qāf, Ṭā, Bā, Jīm, Dāl (spelled as قطب جد).
If one of the Qalqalah letters has a Shaddah at the end, pronounce Shaddah with Qalqalah, e.g. TABB (تَبّ), BIL-ḤAQQ (بِالْحَقّ).
Do not apply qalqalah to any letters except the five Qalqalah letters.

lesson 09 - Madd

description:
The three letters of Madd (elongation) are Alif, Waw, and Ya.
Elongate each of madd letters to one Alif in length.
If Alif preceded by Fatha, it will become a madd letter, e.g. BĀ(بَا), TĀ(تَا), THĀ(ثَا).
If Waw Saakin preceded by Dammah, it will become a madd letter, e.g. BŪ(بُو), TŪ(تُو), THŪ(ثُو).
If Ya Saakin preceded by Kasrah, it will become a madd letter, e.g. BĪ(بِي), TĪ(تِي), THĪ(ثِي).
Avoid unclear (majhool) pronunciation of Waw and Ya Maddah.
If Madd letter is followed by Hamzah(ء), it should be elongated to two Alifs, e.g. JĀ'A(جَاءَ), QĀLŪ ĀMANNAA(قَالُوا آمَنَّا).
If Madd letters are followed by Sukoon or Shaddah, they will elongated to three Alifs, e.g. ĀL-ĀNA(الآنَ), DĀBBATIN(دَابَّةٍ).
If the Sukoon after madd letters is due to stopping, then elongating the Madd letters by one, two, or three Alifs is acceptable, e.g. AL-KITĀB(الْكِتَابْ), AL-'ĀLAMĪN(الْعَالَمِينَ), YUNFIQŪN(يُنْفِقُونْ).
These madd rules applies if these signs come after long voices: Wa ṣaddaqa bihī ulā'ika(وَصَدَّقَ بِهِ أُولَئِكَ), hā'ulā'i(هَؤُلَاءِ).

lesson 10 -  leen letters

description:
Soft letters (leen) should be pronounced gently without elongation.
There are two soft letters: Waw (و) and Ya (ي).
If Waw Saakin preceded by Fatha, it will become leen letter e.g. BAW (بَوْ), TAW (تَوْ), THAW (ثَوْ).
If Ya Saakin preceded by Fatha, it will become leen letter e.g. BAI (بَيْ), TAI (تَيْ), THAI (ثَيْ).
Avoid unclear (majhool) pronunciation of soft letters.
If the Sukoon after leen letters is due to stopping, then it can be elongated by one, two, or three Alifs, e.g. KHAWF (خَوْف), QURAYSH (قُرَيْش).

lesson 11 - Heavy letters

description:
Heavy letters (ḥurūf Mustaʿliyah) (حروف مستعليه) are pronounced with a full (heavy) tone.
The heavy letters are seven: Khā, Ṣād, Ḍād, Ghain, Ṭā, Qāf, Ẓāad (spelled as "خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ").
They are always pronounced heavily, whether with fatha, kasra, or damma.
While pronouncing them, the root of the tongue lifts toward the palate of the mouth.
Do not round the lips while pronouncing heavy letters.
The letters Alif, Lām, and Rā’ sometimes sound heavy and sometimes light; they are known as Shibah Mustaʿliyah (partly heavy letters).

lesson 12 - Heavy and Light Alif

description:
If a heavy letter precedes Alif, the Alif is pronounced heavily, e.g. QĀLA(قَالَ).
Do not round the lips while pronouncing heavy Alif, e.g. TĀBA(طَابَ).
If Alif is preceded by a light letter, it is pronounced light, e.g. TĀBA(تَابَ).
Do not lower the lips when pronouncing light Alif.like :KANA(كَانَ)
When pronouncing a heavy Alif, maintain its heaviness from start to finish (e.g., QĀLA – قَالَ، وَلَا – WALĀ, الضَّالِّينَ – WALĀḌḌĀLLĪN).

lesson 13 - Heavy and Light Laam

description:
If Fatha or Dammah precedes the word (ALLĀH) or (ALLĀHUMMA), it is read heavily, e.g. ALLĀHU(اللَّهُ), ALLĀHUMMA(اللَّهُمَّ).
Do not make the light letter before heavy LĀM heavy, e.g. TĀLLĀHI(تَاللَّهِ).
Never make Hā (ه) sound heavy after a heavy Lām (like in إِنَّ اللَّهَ – INNALLĀHA). Especially when stopping (e.g., إِنَّ اللَّهْ – INNALLĀH).
Do not lighten the first sound of heavy LĀM, e.g. KHATAMALLĀHU(خَتَمَ اللَّهُ), ‘ALIMALLĀHU(عَلِمَ اللَّهُ).
If Kasrah precedes word (ALLĀH) or (ALLĀHUMMA), read it lightly, e.g. BILLĀHI(بِاللَّهِ), QULILLĀHUMMA(قُلِ اللَّهُمَّ).
Any Lām other than ALLĀH/ALLĀHUMMA is pronounced light. ḌALLA(ضَلَّ), ZALLA(زَلَّ).
Do not pronounce the first sound of light LĀM heavily, e.g. BISMILLĀHI(بِسْمِ اللَّهِ).

lesson 14 - Heavy and Light Raa

description:
Rā’ is pronounced heavy (tafkhīm) when it has Fatha or Dammah.
Rā’ is pronounced light (tarqīq) when it has Kasrah.
If Rā’ is sākin:
It is heavy if the letter before it has Fatha or Dammah.
It is light if the letter before it has Kasrah.
If a temporary sukoon occurs because of stopping (waqf), the rule depends on the vowel before the Rā’.
Do not exaggerate the heaviness or lightness of the Rā’; pronounce it according to its rule only.

lesson 15 - Ghunnah

description:
Ghunnah is the nasal sound produced from the nose.
The letters of Ghunnah are Nūn (ن) and Mīm (م).
Give Ghunnah whenever Nūn or Mīm has Shaddah.
Examples:
إِنَّ
عَمَّ
If Mīm Sākin (مْ) is followed by Bā (ب) or Mīm (م), read it with Ghunnah.
Examples:
عَلَيْكُم بِوَكِيلٍ
عَلَيْكُم مُرْسَلُونَ
If Mīm Sākin (مْ) is followed by any letter other than Bā or Mīm, there is no Ghunnah.
Example:
لَهُم جَنَّاتٌ
If Nūn Sākin (نْ) or Tanwīn is followed by any letter except the six throat letters (ء هـ ع ح غ خ) and Lām (ل) and Rā (ر), it is read with Ghunnah.
Example:
مُنذِرٌ
If Nūn Sākin or Tanwīn is followed by the six throat letters (ء هـ ع ح غ خ) or Lām (ل) or Rā (ر), there is no Ghunnah.
Examples:
مِنْ آمَنَ
مِنْ رَبِّهِ
The four words in which there is no Ghunnah despite the spelling are:
الدُّنْيَا
قِنْوَانٌ
صِنْوَانٌ
بُنْيَانٌ

lesson 16 - Huroof-e-Muqattaʿāt (Disjoint Letters)

description:
These letters are recited individually (separately).
There are 14 such letters, found at the beginning of 29 surahs.
They are recited with two types of Madd.
Letters made up of two components are prolonged by one Alif (2 counts).
Example:
طه (Ṭā Hā)
Letters made up of three components are prolonged by three Alifs (6 counts).
Examples:
ق (Qāf)
ن (Nūn)
The word Alif itself is recited without Madd.
Examples:
الم (Alif Lām Mīm)
الر (Alif Lām Rā)
