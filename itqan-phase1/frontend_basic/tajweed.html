Itqān (إتقان) — AI Learning Platform Architecture PRD

The definitive Product Requirements Document (PRD) and pedagogical blueprint for an AI-native, conversational Tajweed teacher and pronunciation laboratory. This specification establishes the permanent foundation upon which every module, lesson, interactive audio component, AI Wav2Vec2 CTC forced-alignment workflow, and Spaced Repetition engine is engineered.

Axiom

15 Pedagogical Learning Science Principles

Every screen, interactive audio widget, and AI correction dialog in Itqān derives directly from modern learning science and traditional Quranic Talaqqi (oral transmission):

1. Active Recall
Learners retrieve articulatory rules from memory via audio quizzes rather than passively scanning textbook rules.
2. Retrieval Practice
Frequent low-stakes audio challenges reinforce neural pathways for rapid motor tongue/lip positioning.
3. Deliberate Practice
Focused micro-drills targeting an individual's specific acoustic error boundaries (e.g., Qalqalah rebound timing).
4. Mastery Learning
An 85% Confidence Score threshold is mandatory before progressing to downstream dependent rules.
5. Progressive Disclosure
Rules are revealed in atomic units—starting with Makhaarij before introducing complex Madd durations.
6. Cognitive Load Theory
Eliminates extraneous text; interfaces present only essential anatomical cues and real-time audio waveforms.
7. Dual Coding
Combines auditory Qari master recitations simultaneously with color-coded Uthmani script visual cues.
8. Spaced Repetition (SRS)
An adapted Ebbinghaus exponential decay algorithm schedules rule revisions right before forgetting occurs.
9. Immediate Feedback
Sub-second Wav2Vec2 CTC alignment provides instant character-level diagnostic feedback post-recitation.
10. Error-Based Learning
Mistakes are treated as diagnostic learning moments; punitive red markers are replaced by amber guidance.
11. Interleaving
Mixes different rule categories (e.g., Ghunnah with Qalqalah) during practice to build robust auditory discrimination.
12. Scaffolding
Initial attempts provide full reference audio shadowing; subsequent attempts remove audio prompts as mastery builds.
13. Microlearning
Lessons are compartmentalized into 3-to-5 minute interactive blocks fitting easily into daily devotional routines.
14. Experiential Learning
Learners experiment with breath pressure and vocal tract shaping inside an interactive pronunciation laboratory.
15. Intrinsic Motivation
Grounded in the authentic Hadith that one who struggles with Quranic recitation receives double the divine reward.
Part 01

Platform Identity

1.1 Complete Product & Pedagogical Specification
1. Purpose
To democratize authentic Quranic recitation (*Talaqqi*) globally by providing a 24/7 personal AI Tajweed teacher that diagnoses acoustic pronunciation errors with scholarly precision.
2. Educational Reasoning
Traditional Tajweed cannot be learned from static textbooks; it requires continuous oral modeling, real-time feedback, and empathetic correction to build subconscious motor memory.
3. UX Reasoning
The interface replaces cluttered academic documentation with a serene, conversational companion that guides the learner step-by-step through speech interactions.
4. User Journey
A Muslim learner enters with recitation hesitation -> completes a voice baseline assessment -> enters a personalized daily 5-minute practice loop -> graduates with certified *Itqān* proficiency.
5. Interaction Flow
Home Dashboard -> Daily Review Prompt -> Interactive Voice Recitation Studio -> Sub-second Diagnostic Card -> Empathetic Audio Tip -> Retry or Mastery Advancement.
6. Potential Edge Cases
Learners reciting with regional dialects or background acoustic noise; children with higher vocal pitch formants; advanced reciters requiring strict 0.2s duration tolerances.
7. Design Recommendations
Use Midnight Blue dark mode (`#060b13`) to evoke tranquility; strictly prohibit punitive red words; use Emerald Green (`#10b981`) for mastery and Amber (`#f59e0b`) for gentle refinement.
8. Future Scalability
Architecture supports expanding from Hafs 'an 'Asim to all 10 Qira'at canonical recitations and integrating live human Sheikh verification for borderline confidence scores.

Part 03

Learning Architecture & 7-Module Curriculum Hierarchy

The educational data structure operates across an 8-layer pedagogical hierarchy: Course → Module → Chapter → Lesson → Section → Interaction → Exercise → Assessment. Below is the complete specification of the 7 Tajweed Modules implemented in the platform:

Module 1: Foundations of Recitation

Prerequisite: None
This module sets the spiritual and physical groundwork for reading the Qur'an before introducing rules.
  • The Aadaab (Etiquette): Prerequisites like Wudhu, intention, volume control, and the required opening supplications (Ta'awwuth and Basmalah).
  • Makhaarij (Places of Origin): How to find the origin of a letter using a Sukoon and Fathah.
  • The 7 Groups of Articulation: Aerial, Guttural, Velar, Palatal, Dental, Gingival, and Labial letters, plus the Liquids and the Tip of the Tongue.

Module 2: Letter Characteristics & Pronunciation

Prerequisite: Module 1 Mastery
This module introduces the core definition of Tajweed and special rules for specific individual letters.
  • Introduction to Tajweed: Defining the practice of pronouncing every letter with its articulative qualities.
  • Qalqala (The Echoing Letters) (ق ط ب ج د): The rules for reading ق ط ب ج د with a jerking sound, and how stopping affects them.
  • The Rule of Laam (ل): When to read the name of Allah with a full mouth (heavy) vs. an empty mouth (light), including the exception for Laam Mushaddadah.
  • The Rule of Raa (ر): The 8 comprehensive conditions for pronouncing Raa with a full or thin mouth, covering vowels, Sukoon, Shaddah, and stopping rules.

Module 3: The Rules of Noon and Meem

Prerequisite: Module 2 Mastery
This module focuses heavily on nasalization (Ghunnah) and the most common recurring letters.
  • Noon and Meem Mushaddadah: The baseline rule for 2-Harakaat nasalization when these letters carry a Shaddah.
  • The 3 Rules of Meem Saakin (مْ): Ikhfa Shafawi (with Ba), Idghaam Shafawi (with Meem), and Ithaar Shafawi (remaining 26 letters).
  • The Rules of Noon Saakin (نْ) and Tanween:
    • Ikhfa: The 15 letters causing a light nasal sound.
    • Ithaar: The 6 Huroof Halqiyah (throat letters) causing clear pronunciation without Ghunnah.
    • Idghaam: The merging rules for ي ن م و (with Ghunnah) and ل ر (without Ghunnah).

Module 4: Advanced Assimilation (Idghaam)

Prerequisite: Module 3 Mastery
This module tackles how specific neighboring letters merge into one another.
  • Idghaam Mithlayn: The assimilation of two identical letters (e.g., a Saakin letter merging into a Mushaddadah letter of the same kind).
  • Idghaam Mutaqaaribayn: The assimilation of letters with similar origins (e.g., ق into ك, or ب into م).

Module 5: The Rules of Elongation (Madd)

Prerequisite: Module 4 Mastery
This module teaches rhythm, breath control, and proper prolongation of syllables.
  • Huroof-ul-Madd (ا ، و ، ي): The baseline letters of elongation (ا ، و ، ي).
  • Maddul Asli: The original 2-Harakaat elongation.
  • Maddul Muttasil & Munfasil: Joined (4-6 Harakaat) vs. Detached (3-5 Harakaat) Madd involving a Hamzah.
  • Maddul Laazim: Compulsory 6-Harakaat elongation for disjointed letters (Huroof Muqattaʿat).
  • Maddul Aaridh: The 2-to-5 Harakaat elongation caused by an abrupt stop.

Module 6: Connecting Words (Alif-Laam)

Prerequisite: Module 5 Mastery
This module covers the flow of reading between definite and indefinite nouns.
  • The Sun Letters (حروف شمسية): The 14 letters where the Laam (ل) is assimilated and not pronounced.
  • The Moon Letters (حروف قمرية): The 14 letters where the Laam (ل) is pronounced clearly as a Saakin.

Module 7: Stopping and Prostration

Prerequisite: Module 6 Mastery
This final module guides the reciter on how to manage their breath and physical acts of worship during recitation.
  • The Rules of Stopping (Waqf): How to change short vowels to a Saakin, and how to handle Fathatain or Madd when stopping.
  • Symbols Denoting Pauses: A breakdown of symbols like م (Compulsory), ط (Necessary), and others that dictate reading flow.
  • Verses of Prostration: The 14 indexed verses where a Sajdah is required during recitation.

Reusable Learning Component System

4.1 Audio Studio & Pronunciation Laboratory Component Library
1. Purpose
To provide a standardized library of 14 interactive UI/audio components that content designers compose into lessons without writing ad-hoc code.
2. Educational Reasoning
Consistent interaction patterns reduce extraneous cognitive load, allowing working memory to focus entirely on auditory discrimination and vocal tract control.
3. UX Reasoning
Modular cards with haptic audio triggers, clear visual states, and touch-optimized hit areas create a seamless mobile-first speech laboratory.

Learner views a Concept Card -> explores an Interactive Anatomical Diagram -> plays a Listen & Compare minimal pair -> records in the Audio Laboratory -> receives an AI Feedback Card.

All components accept JSON-driven props, allowing headless CMS publishing of thousands of Quranic verses without UI redesigns.

Part 06

Independent Practice System & AI Selection Engine

6.1 Spaced Repetition (SRS) & Adaptive Exercise Routing
1. Purpose
To maintain long-term retention across all learned Tajweed rules by serving custom daily practice decks before forgetting occurs.
2. Educational Reasoning
Ebbinghaus forgetting curve proves that without spaced retrieval practice, motor articulation rules decay within 14 days of initial learning.
3. UX Reasoning
A single 1-Click "Begin Daily Review (5 min)" button removes decision fatigue, automatically serving 3 targeted verses based on weak areas.
4. User Journey
Learner opens app -> clicks Daily Review -> recites 3 adaptive verses -> AI updates forgetting curve intervals -> streak increases.
5. Interaction Flow
AI queries PostgreSQL `MASTERY` table -> selects rules where `NextReviewDate <= CURRENT_TIMESTAMP` or `ConfidenceScore < 60%` -> renders audio practice loop.
6. Potential Edge Cases
Learners who skip practice for 30 days (avoid punishment; gently schedule top 5 foundational refreshers); offline practice queue synchronization.
7. Design Recommendations
Provide 6 distinct modes: Daily Practice, Weak Area Drill, Revision Deck, Random Verse Challenge, Custom Chapter Drill, and Timed Endurance Mode.
8. Future Scalability
Integrates with **Khatma Mode** (Module 7)—when a learner makes a mistake during open Quranic reading, that rule immediately enters tomorrow's SRS review deck.

Part 08

AI Teacher Persona & Conversational Intelligence

8.1 Empathetic Sheikh Persona & 3-Attempt Frustration Management
1. Purpose
To embody a compassionate, patient, and precise Tajweed scholar who guides learners through encouragement rather than judgment.
2. Educational Reasoning
Anxiety impairs motor speech performance; an encouraging persona lowers affective filter and encourages repeated vocal experimentation.
3. UX Reasoning
Strict ban on punitive words ("Wrong", "Incorrect", "Failed"); replaces them with constructive amber diagnostic cues and analogies.
4. User Journey
Learner makes error -> AI praises effort -> explains specific phonetic cause -> models correction -> offers calm retry attempt.
5. Interaction Flow
3-Attempt Frustration Protocol: If a learner fails 3 consecutive attempts on a rule, AI pauses recording -> offers a breathing prompt -> plays a slow reference audio -> breaks down the letter in isolation.
6. Potential Edge Cases
Learners getting frustrated by technical phonetic terms; AI automatically simplifies explanations using memorable physical analogies.
7. Design Recommendations
Use warm, conversational tone; cite authentic Hadiths on patience; use Amber ("Needs Review") badges instead of red "X" icons.
8. Future Scalability
Stores conversation memory per user profile, allowing the AI to recall past difficult rules ("Remember how we mastered Ikhfa last week? This follows the same lip placement").

Assessment Framework & Mistake Taxonomy

10.1 6-Category Diagnostic Error Classification

All pronunciation errors are classified into 6 standardized diagnostic buckets to trigger precise corrective explanations:

Taxonomy Category Specific Error Example AI Detection Method Automated Remediation Action
1. Makhraj (Origin) Pronouncing Sin (س) instead of Saad (ص). Levenshtein ASR character distance mismatch. Displays cross-section diagram showing tongue elevation against posterior palate.
2. Sifaat (Quality) Omitting Qalqalah echo burst on stop. Absence of high-frequency energy rebound. Triggers 30-second Qalqalah rebounding analogy drill.
3. Ahkaam - Madd Holding Maddul Muttasil for only 2 beats instead of 4. Forced alignment duration < 1.8 seconds. Displays Harakaat counting bar with visual rhythm pulse.
4. Ahkaam - Ghunnah Omitting 2-beat nasalization on Noon Mushaddad. Nasal band energy ratio drop. Directs focus to nasal resonance without excessive lip tension.
5. Waqf - Vowel Pronouncing final Fathah at sentence stop. Audio energy detected after final consonant timestamp. Explains mandatory conversion of final short vowels to Sukoon at Waqf.
6. Waqf - Breathing Unlawful respiration pause inside connected word. Silence interval > 0.4s inside word boundary. Advises proper breath planning before initiating Quranic phrase.
