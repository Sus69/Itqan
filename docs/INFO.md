# THE AADAAB OF RECITING THE HOLY QUR’AN

### تلاوتِ قرآنِ مجید کے آداب

* The reciter of the Holy Qur’an must perform the ritual ablution (wudhu).
* The intention when reciting the Holy Qur’an should be to gain the pleasure of Allah.
* The voice should not be raised to such an extent where your recital will disturb others who are also engaged in some form of worship.
* The reciter of the Holy Qur’an must sit in a dignified position facing the Ka’bah.
* When commencing with the recitation of the Holy Qur’an — start by reciting:

### أَعُوذُ بِاللّٰهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ

> “I seek Allah’s protection from Satan, the accursed.”

And thereafter recite:

### بِسْمِ اللّٰهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

> “In the name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.”



# The Place of Origin of the Arabic Letters

To know the place of origin (**Makhraj**) of any letter of the Arabic alphabet:

* Place a **Sukoon (ْ)** on the letter.
* Precede it with an **Alif (ا)** carrying a **Fathah (َ)**.

**Example:**

**اَبْ**

This will give us the place of origin (Makhraj) of the letter **ب**.

\---

## Places of Origin (Makhaarij)

|Group|Letters|Place of Origin|
|-|-|-|
|**The Aerial Letters**|ا ، و ، ي|Originates from the emptiness (open cavity) of the mouth.|
|**The Guttural Letters**|ء ، هـ|Originates from the back of the throat (larynx).|
||ع ، ح|Originates from the centre (middle) of the throat.|
||غ ، خ|Originates from the upper portion of the throat.|
|**Velar Letters**|ق ، ك|The back of the tongue rises and touches the soft palate.|
|**Palatal Letters**|ج ، ش ، ي|The centre of the tongue touches the upper palate.|



## Continued: Places of Origin (Makhaarij)

|Group|Letters|Place of Origin|
|-|-|-|
|**The letter ض**|ض|The turned side of the tongue touches the gums of the upper back teeth.|
|**The Liquids**|ل ، ر ، ن|Originates when the tongue touches the upper hard palate.|
|**The Dental Letters**|ت ، د ، ط|Originates when the tip of the tongue touches the gums of the upper two front teeth.|
|**The Gingival Letters**|ث ، ذ ، ظ|Originates when the tip of the tongue touches the edge of the upper two front teeth.|
|**Tip of the Tongue**|س ، ص ، ز|Originates when the tip of the tongue rises towards the upper palate and touches the gums behind the upper two front teeth.|
|**The Labial Letters**|ب ، م|Originates from the lips.|
||ف|Originates when the inner portion of the bottom lip meets the edge of the two upper front teeth.|



# TAJWEED

Reciting the Holy Qur’an with **Tajweed** means to pronounce every letter with all its articulative qualities such as the correct prolongation, merging, conversion, distinctness, and pauses. Reciting the Qur’an with Tajweed allows the reciter to emphasise the accent, phonetics, rhythm, and temper of the Qur'anic recitation.

# NOON AND MEEM MUSHADDADAH

When the letters **ن** (Noon) and **م** (Meem) have a **Shaddah (ّ)** on them, they are recited with **Ghunnah** (nasalization). The nasalization should not exceed the duration of **two Harakaat** (approximately 2–3 seconds).

|27|70|وَلَا تَحْزَنْ عَلَيْهِمْ وَضِقْ بِمَا يَمْكُرُونَ|**مّ**|
|7|11|ثُمَّ قُلْنَا لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ اسْجُدُوا|**مّ**|





# THE RULE OF THE LETTER LAAM (ل)

When a letter with **Fathah (َ)** or **Dhammah (ُ)** appears before the name of **Allah (اللّٰه)**, the **Laam** is pronounced with a **broad (heavy)** sound, also called a **full mouth** pronunciation.

### Examples

|Surah No.|Verse No.|Extract from Verse|Vowel Sign|
|:-:|:-:|-|:-:|
|5|114|قَالَ عِيسَى ابْنُ مَرْيَمَ اللَّهُمَّ|**Fathah (َ)**|
|4|171|إِنَّمَا الْمَسِيحُ عِيسَى ابْنُ مَرْيَمَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ|**Dhammah (ُ)**|

\---

When a letter with **Kasrah (ِ)** appears before the name of **Allah (اللّٰه)**, the **Laam** is pronounced with a **thin (light)** sound, also called an **empty mouth** pronunciation.

### Examples

|Surah No.|Verse No.|Extract from Verse|Vowel Sign|
|:-:|:-:|-|:-:|
|40|78|لِرَسُولٍ أَن يَأْتِيَ بِآيَةٍ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ|**Kasrah (ِ)**|
|4|35|يُوَفِّقِ اللَّهُ بَيْنَهُمَا|**Kasrah (ِ)**|

\---

## Exception: Laam Mushaddadah (لّ)

The **Laam Mushaddadah (لّ)** is **always pronounced with a thin (light) sound**, regardless of the preceding vowel.

# THE RULE OF MEEM SAAKIN (مْ)

There are **three rules** regarding **Meem Saakin (مْ)**:

1. **Ikhfa Shafawi**
2. **Idghaam Shafawi**
3. **Ithaar Shafawi**

\---

# 1\. IKHFA SHAFAWI – MEEM SAAKIN (مْ)

When the letter **Ba (ب)** appears after a **Meem Saakin (مْ)**, there will be **Ikhfa Shafawi**. It is pronounced with a **light nasal sound (Ghunnah)** for a duration of **2 Harakaat (approximately 2–3 seconds).**

### Example

|Surah No.|Verse No.|Extract from Verse|Rule|
|:-:|:-:|-|-|
|34|8|أَفْتَرَىٰ عَلَى اللَّهِ كَذِبًا أَمْ بِهِ جِنَّةٌ|**Ikhfa Shafawi (مْ + ب)**|

\---

# 2\. IDGHAAM SHAFAWI – MEEM SAAKIN (مْ)

If after a **Meem Saakin (مْ)** there appears a **Meem Mushaddadah (مّ)**, **Idghaam** will occur.

In other words, the **two Meems merge into one** and are recited with **Ghunnah (nasalization)**.

### Example

|Surah No.|Verse No.|Extract from Verse|Rule|
|:-:|:-:|-|-|
|16|57|وَلَهُم مَّا يَشْتَهُونَ|**Idghaam Shafawi (مْ + مّ)**|

\---

# 3\. ITHAAR SHAFAWI – MEEM SAAKIN (مْ)

When, after a **Meem Saakin (مْ)**, there appears **any of the remaining 26 Arabic letters** (that is, any letter **other than ب and م**), the rule is **Ithaar Shafawi**.

The **Meem is pronounced clearly**, and **no Ghunnah (nasalization)** occurs.

### Example

|Surah No.|Verse No.|Extract from Verse|Rule|
|:-:|:-:|-|-|
|34|45|وَكَذَّبَ الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ وَمَا بَلَغُوا|**Ithaar Shafawi (مْ followed by any letter other than ب or م)**|

# IDGHAAM – NOON SAAKIN AND TANWEEN

**Idghaam** refers to the **assimilation (merging) of one letter into another**.

The rule of **Idghaam** applies when a **Noon Saakin (نْ)** or **Tanween (ً ٍ ٌ)** is followed by one of the letters:

**ي ن م و**

The succeeding letter is emphasized because of the presence of a **Shaddah (ّ)** and is recited with **Ghunnah (nasalization)**. The Ghunnah should not exceed **2 Harakaat (approximately 2–3 seconds).**

# IDGHAAM MITHLAYN

*(Assimilation of the Same Kind)*

This rule applies when **two identical letters** follow one another. The **first letter is Saakin (ْ)** and the **second letter carries a Shaddah (ّ)**.

When recited, the **Saakin letter is assimilated into the following letter**.

\---

## Examples

|Surah|Verse|Extract from Verse|
|:-:|:-:|-|

|8|72|إِذَا قُمْتُّمْ|

# IDGHAAM MUTAQAARIBAYN

*(Assimilation of Letters with Similar Origin)*

This rule applies when a letter in a word is **Saakin (ْ)** and the following letter carries a **Shaddah (ّ)** or is pronounced very close to the same place of articulation as the Saakin letter.

The **Saakin letter assimilates into the following letter** during recitation.

# IDGHAAM MITHLAYN

*(Assimilation of the Same Kind)*

This rule applies when **two identical letters** follow one another. The **first letter is Saakin (ْ)** and the **second letter carries a Shaddah (ّ)**.

When recited, the **Saakin letter is assimilated into the following letter**.

# THE LETTER RAA (ر)

## 1\. Raa with Fathah (َ) or Dhammah (ُ)

A **Raa (ر)** with a **Fathah (َ)** or **Dhammah (ُ)** is pronounced with a **full (heavy) mouth**.

### Examples

|Surah|Verse|Extract from Verse|
|:-:|:-:|-|
|2|16|فَمَا رَبِحَت تِّجَارَتُهُمْ|
|2|28|تَكْفُرُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَكُنتُمْ|

\---

## 2\. Raa with Kasrah (ِ)

A **Raa (ر)** with a **Kasrah (ِ)** is pronounced with a **thin (light) mouth**.

### Examples

|Surah|Verse|Extract from Verse|
|:-:|:-:|-|
|2|54|لَكُمْ عِندَ بَارِئِكُمْ|
|2|75|كَلَامَ اللَّهِ ثُمَّ يُحَرِّفُونَهُ|

\---

## 3\. Raa Saakin (رْ) preceded by Fathah (َ) or Dhammah (ُ)

When a **Fathah (َ)** or **Dhammah (ُ)** comes before a **Raa Saakin (رْ)**, the **Raa Saakin** is pronounced with a **full (heavy) mouth**.

### Examples

|Surah|Verse|Extract from Verse|
|:-:|:-:|-|
|2|7|أَبْصَارِهِمْ وَعَلَىٰ|
|2|252|وَإِنَّكَ لَمِنَ الْمُرْسَلِينَ|

\---

## 4\. Raa Saakin (رْ) preceded by Kasrah (ِ)

If a **Kasrah (ِ)** comes before a **Raa Saakin (رْ)**, the **Raa Saakin** is pronounced with a **thin (light) mouth**.

### Example

|Surah|Verse|Extract from Verse|
|:-:|:-:|-|
|2|6|تُنذِرْهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ|

# MADDUL MUTTASIL

*(The Joined Madd)*

When a **Huroof-ul-Madd (ا، و، ي)** is followed by a **Hamzah (ء)** **in the same word**, the Madd is called **Maddul Muttasil**.

**Length of recitation:** **4 to 6 Harakaat** (long; approximately 4–6 seconds).

**Length of recitation:** **3 to 5 Harakaat** (intermediate; approximately 3–5 seconds).

# THE SUN LETTERS

When the **definite article (ال)** is attached to an **indefinite word**, the **Tanween (ً ٍ ٌ)** changes into a **short vowel**.

**Example:**

* شَجَرَةٌ → الشَّجَرَةُ

An indefinite word beginning with a **Sun Letter**, when the definite article **(ال)** is attached, results in the **Laam (ل)** **not being pronounced**.

The **Alif (ا)** of the definite article **(ال)** is recited and merges with the **Sun Letter**, which then carries a **Shaddah (ّ)**.

**Example:**

* الشَّجَرَةُ *(pronounced ash-shajarah, not al-shajarah)*

The **Shaddah (ّ)** indicates that the pronunciation of the letter must be **strengthened (geminated)**.

However, if the definite article **(ال)** is **preceded by another word or letter**, the **Hamzat al-Wasl (ا)** is **not pronounced**.

**Example:**

* تَحْتَ الشَّجَرَةِ

# THE MOON LETTERS

When the **definite article (ال)** is attached to an **indefinite word**, the **Tanween (ً ٍ ٌ)** changes into a **short vowel**.

**Example:**

* مَسْجِدٌ → الْمَسْجِدُ

An indefinite word beginning with a **Moon Letter**, when the definite article **(ال)** is attached, results in the **Laam (ل)** being **pronounced clearly as Laam Saakin (لْ)**.

The **Alif (ا)** in the definite article is **Hamzatul Wasl**. It is written, but **is not pronounced** when it is preceded by another word or letter.

**Example:**

* فِي الْمَسْجِدِ

However, if the definite noun appears as the **first word of a sentence**, the **Alif (ا)** is pronounced.

## THE RULES OF STOPPING

If any of these signs **(ـُ / ـِ / ـٌ / ـٍ)** appear on the last letter of a word when a stop is required, then the last letter is read as **SAAKIN (ْ)**.

**Urdu:**

اگر اعراب میں سے کوئی نشان **(ـُ / ـِ / ـٌ / ـٍ)** کسی لفظ کے آخری حرف پر ہو اور وہاں ٹھہرنا ضروری ہو تو آخری حرف ساکن ہوگا۔

### Examples

|Surah|Verse|Extract from Verse|
|-:|-:|-|
|88|4|نَارًا حَامِيَةً|
|7|24|إِلَىٰ حِينٍ|
|15|6|إِنَّكَ لَمَجْنُونٌ|
|15|8|إِذًا مُنظَرِينَ|
|88|1|هَلْ أَتَىٰكَ حَدِيثُ الْغَاشِيَةِ|
|89|27|يَا أَيَّتُهَا النَّفْسُ الْمُطْمَئِنَّةُ|

\---

**Urdu:**

تاہم اگر آخری حرف پر **دوزبر (ً)** ہو یا **مد** ہو تو آخری حرف اس طرح پڑھا جائے گا جیسے اس پر ایک زبر **(َ)** ہو۔
